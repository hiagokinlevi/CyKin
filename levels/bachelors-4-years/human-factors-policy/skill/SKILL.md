## Forty-chapter bachelor's route

This is the four-year **bachelor's course** in human factors and cyber policy. The book follows synthetic student **Nadia Farouk**, supervised by **Dr Owen Prescott** at the fictional **Fenmoor University**, studying security reporting at the fictional **Ashcombe Community Library Network**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Reasoning, computing, programming, writing, security literacy, discrete structures, email protocols, data and ethics | lab_base (1), lab_count (3), lab_graph (6), lab_csv (8), lab_sql (9); EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Operating systems, statistics, identity, privacy, measurement, architecture, cryptography, research methods, theory and cases | lab_ci (12), lab_median (15); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | System models, dependencies, methods, controls, Laboratory I, change over time, verification, failure analysis, operations and Laboratory II | lab_funnel (21), lab_barriers (23), lab_button (25), lab_compare (26), lab_factors (28), lab_reply (30); EX061–EX090 |
| 31–40 | Advanced cases, interdisciplinary work, comparative project, capstone, community practice, evidence review and thesis | lab_networks (34), lab_capstone (39); EX091–EX120 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 98% of flagged emails harmless at a 1-in-1,000 base rate; 18/120 = 15.0% (9.7–22.5%); funnel 24 of 100; top barrier 45/84 = 54%; button 6 s vs forwarding 40 s; repeat reporting 65% vs 30%; Ashcombe median 14 min and 86% reported vs Dunmore 70 min and 52%; knowing how to report 41% to 81%.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Awareness and behaviour claims must cite real sources and name their limits.
- Phishing simulations only with written approval, consent at the organisational level, no punishment of individuals, aggregate reporting and support for staff; never harvest real credentials or personal data.
