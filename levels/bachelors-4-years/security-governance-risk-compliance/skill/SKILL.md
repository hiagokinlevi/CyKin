## Forty-chapter bachelor's route

This is the four-year **bachelor's course** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic student **Sofia Reyes**, tutored by **Dr. Malik Haddad** at the fictional **Brightwater College**, building the GRC programme of the fictional **Elmfield Housing Association**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Reasoning, systems, programming, writing, literacy, structures, networks, data and ethics | lab_sqlite (9); EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Operating systems, statistics, identity, privacy, measurement, architecture, cryptography, research and GRC theory | lab_wilson (12), lab_patch (15); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Models, suppliers, risk methods, controls, laboratories, gap assessment, verification, failure analysis and operations | lab_tier (22), lab_score (23), lab_gap (26); EX061–EX090 |
| 31–40 | Advanced cases, interfaces, research II, comparison, capstone, practice, evidence review and thesis | EX091–EX120 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): register top risks tied at 12 with two unowned; acknowledgement repairs 60% (42–75%) vs housing 87%; patch median 21 days, 67% within 30; three high-tier suppliers; matrix top misdirected data vs expected-loss top ransomware 25k; CSF gap overall 54% with Detect weakest at 25%.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
