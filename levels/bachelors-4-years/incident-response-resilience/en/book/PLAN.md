# Principal book plan — Incident response and cyber resilience / Bachelor's course / English

**One book for this course and language.** 40 curricular subjects are its chapters. Duration: 4 years; planned effort: approximately 3200 hours. Audience: Broad security and computing students. Entry knowledge: Mathematics, computing and communication foundations. Intended outcome: compare theories and methods, evaluate evidence and design defensible solutions in incident response and cyber resilience.

**Editorial state:** structured outline; manuscript not yet authored. English reference edition.

## Book requirements

A finished book must contain at least 300 actual A4 pages and 50 substantive exercises with worked results. The current 1200-page budget is a plan, not measured pages. Every chapter needs teaching, definitions, a worked example, practical activity, errors and limits, exercises and a synthesis. The final appendix holds answers keyed to the exercise IDs. Cite verifiable sources, label synthetic cases, and review for technical accuracy, rights and accessibility.

## Primary source starting points

- [NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final) — current incident-response recommendations within cybersecurity risk management; it supersedes Rev. 2.
- [NIST SP 800-184](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/184/final) — recovery planning, testing and improvement.
- [NIST SP 800-84](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/84/final) — exercise design and evaluation; a 2006 publication whose applicability must be checked during authorship.
- [NIST SP 800-86](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final) — forensic techniques in response; a 2006 publication whose technical details need current verification.

These are source leads, not a finished chapter bibliography. Cite the relevant primary source beside each factual claim in the authored manuscript and identify any fictional case as synthetic.
