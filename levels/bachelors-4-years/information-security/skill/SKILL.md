## Forty-chapter bachelor's route

This is the four-year **bachelor's course** in information security. The book follows synthetic student **Aisha Rahimi**, tutored by **Dr. Neil Carrow** at the fictional **Pennick University**, working with the fictional **Ashfield Credit Union**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Course, security objectives, information, computers, networks, Python, statistics, writing, law and ethics | EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Governance, roles, policies, risk, assets, access control, authentication, access review, cryptography and awareness | lab_access (18); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Vulnerabilities, configuration, logging, incidents, backups, passwords, suppliers, physical security, measurement and audit | lab_vulns (21), lab_passwords (26); EX061–EX090 |
| 31–40 | Change, business case, group project, placement, research methods, dissertation and viva | EX091–EX120 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): Access review of 140 synthetic accounts: 10 leavers still active, 13 current staff with access beyond role (6 with admin); 400 open findings: critical 5 of 29 overdue (17.2%), high 27 of 103 (26.2%), medium 57 of 268 (21.3%), 89 overdue in total; salted PBKDF2 gives different hashes for the same password, 6 of 300 synthetic passwords (2.0%) in a breached list.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
