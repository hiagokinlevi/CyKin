## Forty-chapter bachelor's route

This is the four-year **bachelor's course** in data governance. The book follows synthetic student **Amara Okafor**, tutored by **Dr. Paul Lindqvist** at the fictional **Ridgeway University**, working with the fictional **Harbourline Co-operative**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Data basics, organisations, spreadsheets, databases, Python, statistics, writing, law and ethics | EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Governance foundations, roles, policies, quality, metadata, master data, architecture, profiling, rules and issues | lab_profile (18); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Reference codes, security, privacy, sharing, life cycle, retention, analytics, literacy, measurement and maturity | lab_codes (21), lab_retention (26); EX061–EX090 |
| 31–40 | Change, business case, group project, placement, research methods, dissertation and viva | EX091–EX120 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): Profile of 5,000 records: email 82.3%, birth year 68.6%, 2,594 complete on all fields; 89 of 2,000 store codes invalid (4.5%: S41 32, s07 30, S7 16, S00 11); 2,521 of 5,000 records (50.4%) inactive over three years.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
