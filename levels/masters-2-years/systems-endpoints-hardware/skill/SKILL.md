## Eight-subject teaching route

This is the two-year **master's course** for systems, endpoint and hardware security. The book follows synthetic student **Chloé Dubois** of the fictional **Redbourne University**, supervised by **Dr Arjun Mehta**, studying why laptops at the fictional **Halvorsen Engineering** drift from a CIS- and Microsoft-derived security baseline, testing three competing explanations: local admin rights, remote working and department needs.

| Subjects | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Question and literature, baseline seminar, design and ethics | lab_baseline.py (2); EX001–EX020 |
| 4–6 | Competing explanations, proposal and pilot, analysis | lab_pilot.py (5), lab_explain.py (6); EX021–EX038 |
| 7–8 | Writing and limitations, independent review and defense | EX039–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic): 3 of 6 sample laptops drifted, all with extra local admins; pilot 107/120 rows complete (89.2%); drift 55.0% admin vs 15.0% non-admin (RR 3.67, chi-square 86.7), remote 29.0% vs 20.0% (RR 1.45, 6.1), engineering 27.3% vs 21.6% (RR 1.27, 2.1).

## Safety, privacy and information boundaries

- Synthetic configurations, records and data only; no exploit code, payloads, attack procedures or instructions for bypassing controls.
- Standard-library labs that only read synthetic records.
- Devices are pseudonymised, reporting stops at department level, and results are never used to assess individuals.
