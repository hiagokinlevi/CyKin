## Eight-chapter master's route

This is the two-year **master's course** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic student **Lucas Ferreira**, supervised by **Dr. Ingrid Solberg** at the fictional **Marlow Institute of Technology**, studying access reviews at the fictional **Tessaly Logistics**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research question and literature; framework seminar with coverage analysis | lab_map.py (2); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Design and ethics, rival explanations, proposal and sampling pilot | lab_sample.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Data and bootstrap analysis, writing and limitations, review and defense | lab_boot.py (6); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): coverage 80% mapped but 60% evidenced; a clean sample of 60 gives an upper bound 0.049; excess access 0.130 annual versus 0.060 quarterly, difference 0.070 with 95% interval 0.023 to 0.117.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
