## Eight-chapter master's route

This is the two-year **master's course** in privacy and data protection. The book follows synthetic student **Hana Kobayashi**, supervised by **Dr. Owen Pritchard** at the fictional **Ellery Institute of Technology**, studying access-request handling at the fictional **Brookfield Online Retail**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Question, literature and baseline; seminar on the purpose of access | lab_deadline (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Design and ethics, rival explanations, proposal and sample size | lab_power (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Bootstrap and stratified analysis, writing, review and defense | lab_diff (6); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): baseline 74.0% within 30 days (post 29.2%, median 46 days); 211 requests per period detect 0.74 to 0.85; on-time 0.708 to 0.929, difference 0.221 (95% CI 0.154 to 0.287); simple +0.160, complex +0.255; complex share 33% to 17%.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal analysis here is teaching, not legal advice, and a synthetic DPIA or audit is never presented as a real regulatory decision.
