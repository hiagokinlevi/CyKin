## Eight-chapter master's route

This is the two-year **master's course** in human factors and cyber policy. The book follows synthetic student **Sofia Lindqvist**, supervised by **Dr Paul Nakamura** at the fictional **Harrowgate Institute**, studying a password policy change at the fictional **Orchard Lane Hospital Trust**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research question and recorded search; theory seminar on planned behaviour and the compliance budget | EX001–EX014 |
| 3–4 | Mixed-methods design and ethics; competing explanations with an interrupted time series | lab_its.py (4); EX015–EX026 |
| 5–6 | Proposal and pilot; survey change with bootstrap and interview coding | lab_boot.py (6); EX027–EX038 |
| 7–8 | Writing, robustness and limitations; independent review and defense | lab_robust.py (7); EX039–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): level change -18.8 resets per 1,000 accounts (56.9 predicted, 38.1 fitted); writing passwords down 21.0% to 15.0%, bootstrap interval -11.3 to -0.8 points; leave-one-month-out range -19.3 to -17.8.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Awareness and behaviour claims must cite real sources and name their limits.
- Phishing simulations only with written approval, consent at the organisational level, no punishment of individuals, aggregate reporting and support for staff; never harvest real credentials or personal data.
