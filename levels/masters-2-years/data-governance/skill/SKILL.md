## Eight-chapter master's route

This is the two-year **master's course** in data governance. The book follows synthetic student **Tomás Ferreira**, supervised by **Dr. Ingrid Solberg** at the fictional **Kestrel Institute of Technology**, evaluating a stewardship pilot at the fictional **Arden County Council**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Literature method, governance models, design, ethics and reproducibility | EX001–EX020 |
| 4–6 | Rival explanations, measurement pilot and analysis | lab_dupes (5), lab_did (6); EX021–EX038 |
| 7–8 | Writing, limitations, independent review and defense | EX039–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 3,305 synthetic address records: 0 exact duplicates, 384 (11.6%) after normalisation; correction times 24.1 to 14.8 days (stewarded) vs 21.9 to 19.6 (comparison); difference-in-differences -7.0 days, 95% CI -9.1 to -4.8.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
