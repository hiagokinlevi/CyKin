## Fifty-chapter engineering route

This is the five-year **engineering course** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic student **Nadia Okafor**, supervised by **Prof. Tomas Brenner** at the fictional **Kestrel Polytechnic**, engineering the GRC programme of the fictional **Ardwell Power Cooperative**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Foundations, rates, logic, programming, communication and expected loss | lab_ale (7); EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Networks, systems, data, development, security fundamentals and GRC context | lab_register (13); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Requirements, models, threats, residual risk, studios, control design, implementation, measurement and verification | lab_residual (24), lab_kri (29); EX061–EX090 |
| 31–40 | Rollout, reliability, resilience, integration, scale, operations, validation, maintenance and re-verification | lab_layers (32), lab_sample (38); EX091–EX120 |
| 41–50 | Optimisation, research, practice, capstone, review, failure trial, dossier, thesis and defense | lab_budget (41), lab_dossier (48); EX121–EX150 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): total ALE 329k with ransomware first at 90k; register 6 issues, 2 clean rows of 6; vendor misuse residual 80k untreated; pass rates patching 78% and change 72% red, overall 85%; joint failure 0.50% independent vs 2.49% with common cause; 59 clean records bound 5%; best budget set 630k reduction for 190k; one dossier MISMATCH.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
