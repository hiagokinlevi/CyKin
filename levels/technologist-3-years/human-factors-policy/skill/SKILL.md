## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in human factors and cyber policy. The book follows synthetic student **Lucas Ferreira**, taught by **Marta Kowalczyk** at the fictional **Northgate Institute of Technology**, on placement at the fictional **Brightwell Dental Group**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | Systems, networks, programming, data models, literacy, operating systems, identity, architecture and measurement | lab_tally (3), lab_prompts (6), lab_times (9); EX001–EX027 |
| 10–16 | Orientation, assets, data flows, scenarios, methods, guided lab and comparison | lab_scenarios (13), lab_barrier (14), lab_button (15), lab_stagger (16); EX028–EX048 |
| 17–23 | Tooling, controls, monitoring, troubleshooting, integration, automation and evaluation | lab_auto (17), lab_monitor (19), lab_runs (22), lab_eval (23); EX049–EX069 |
| 24–30 | Applied research, team studio, handoff, deployment, field practice and capstone | lab_cross (24), lab_newclinics (27), lab_suppliers (29); EX070–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): prompts 23 to 3 a week; median 11 min to report but 190 min to verdict; top barrier 64/150 = 43%; button 7 s vs 45 s; staggered clinics 80% vs 44%; 9 of 60 reports need human review; reported share 48% to 81%; spoken reminders 58 vs email 39; new clinics 45% then 62% vs 80%.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Awareness and behaviour claims must cite real sources and name their limits.
- Phishing simulations only with written approval, consent at the organisational level, no punishment of individuals, aggregate reporting and support for staff; never harvest real credentials or personal data.
