## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in information security. The book follows synthetic student **Jamal Haddad**, tutored by **Ms. Fiona Grant** at the fictional **Northgate College of Technology**, on placement with the fictional **Riverbend Leisure Trust**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Case, information, operating systems, networks, Python, logs, concepts, hardening, patching and scanning | lab_workstations (8); EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Accounts, MFA, secrets, firewalls, wireless, endpoints, email, backups, encryption and physical security | lab_firewall (14); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Monitoring, incidents, suppliers, procedures, awareness, automation, handover, placement and project | lab_authlog (21); EX061–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 210 synthetic workstations: firewall 95.7%, disk encryption 78.1%, screen lock 89.0%, auto-updates 93.3%, all four 129 (61.4%); 12 firewall rules: allow-any rule 11 makes final deny 12 unreachable, rule 9 duplicates rule 1, rules 6, 9, 10 and 12 unused; 3,271 log lines with 838 failed logins, three external sources 671 (80.1%).

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
