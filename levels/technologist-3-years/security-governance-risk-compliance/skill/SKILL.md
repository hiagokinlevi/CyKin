## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic student **Priya Natarajan**, tutored by **Mr. Oskar Lind** at the fictional **Halden Institute of Technology**, running GRC processes at the fictional **Riverton Dental Group**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | Systems, networks, programming, data, literacy, platforms, identity, architecture and evidence | lab_fresh (9); EX001–EX027 |
| 10–18 | Orientation, context, data flows, scenarios, expected loss, workshop, treatment, tooling and controls | lab_ale (14), lab_tests (17); EX028–EX054 |
| 19–30 | Monitoring, troubleshooting, integration, automation, evaluation, research, studio, handoff, validation, practice and capstone | lab_trend (19), lab_recon (22); EX055–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 3 of 6 evidence items stale; total expected loss 55k with ransomware 27k; South passes 3/3 and East 1/3; vulnerability indicator breaches in June and July (peak 14); 2 leaver and 2 orphan accounts among 7.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
