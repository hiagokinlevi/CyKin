## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in security architecture and engineering. The book follows synthetic student **Tomás Ferreira**, taught by **Ruth Adeyemi** at the fictional **Kestrel Polytechnic**, on placement with **Sam Okafor** at the fictional **Brightwater Logistics**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | Computing, networks, programming, data, literacy, operating systems, identity, architecture basics, measurement | lab_rules.py (3), lab_health.py (9); EX001–EX027 |
| 10–23 | Orientation, assets, data flows, risk scenarios, STRIDE, guided lab, attack trees, tooling, controls, monitoring, troubleshooting, integration, automation, evaluation | lab_manifest.py (15), lab_alerts.py (19), lab_leavers.py (22); EX028–EX069 |
| 24–30 | Applied research, team studio, handoff, deployment, field practice, capstone | lab_reports.py (24); EX070–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 2 of 5 rules open to anywhere; 29/60 devices updated within 30 days; retailer R2 flagged for 5 parcels to one new address; 3 alerts from a synthetic day; 2 leavers still active; prompt reporting 45% before and 81% after.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Architecture advice must cite real standards and name its limits.
- Test or review only systems with written authorisation; report found secrets by location, never by value; never present a framework as proof of outcomes.
