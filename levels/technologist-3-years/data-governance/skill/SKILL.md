## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in data governance. The book follows synthetic student **Ravi Desai**, taught by **Ms. Hannah Moreau** at the fictional **Eastgate Institute of Technology**, working with the fictional **Calder Valley Housing Association**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–10 | Case, data sets, files, SQL, Python, formats, quality concepts, validation, source fixes and codes | lab_jobs (8); EX001–EX030 |
| 11–20 | Duplicates, dictionaries, lineage, roles, procedures, access, pseudonymisation, privacy requests, retention and contractor data | lab_mask (17); EX031–EX060 |
| 21–30 | Contractor performance, dashboards, returns, issues, incidents, automation, documentation, placement and project | lab_emergency (21); EX061–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 6,000 jobs: 167 invalid priorities, 122 completed before raised, 8 both, 5,719 (95.3%) valid; 3 tenants map to 3 pseudonyms, T1001 → 083752155d81 with 3 linked jobs; emergencies within 24h: north 89.1% (median 7.2h), south 74.2% (median 12.5h).

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
