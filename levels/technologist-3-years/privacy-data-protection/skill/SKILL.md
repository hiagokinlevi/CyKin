## Thirty-chapter technologist route

This is the three-year **technologist course** in privacy and data protection. The book follows synthetic student **Aisha Bello**, supervised by **Mr. Tom Reilly** at the fictional **Lindon Technical Institute**, on placement at the fictional **Quayside Fitness**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–9 | Computing, networks, programming, data models, security, systems, access, architecture and measurement | EX001–EX027 |
| 10–15 | Orientation, assets, data flows, risk, access requests and a CCTV guided lab | lab_flows (12), lab_sar (14), lab_cctv (15); EX028–EX045 |
| 16–23 | Impact assessment, tooling, controls, monitoring, troubleshooting, integration, automation and evaluation | lab_scan (17), lab_inactive (22); EX046–EX069 |
| 24–30 | Research, team studio, handoff, deployment, field practice and capstone | EX070–EX090 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): The ad network SDK receives heart-rate zone and location without notice or contract; the first access export covered 47 of 287 records (16%) in 3 of 7 systems; 292 of 500 CCTV clips were past 30 days (oldest 75 days); 3 of 5 shared files held personal data patterns; the deletion job sends 73 warnings, deletes 1,116 accounts and holds 37.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal analysis here is teaching, not legal advice, and a synthetic DPIA or audit is never presented as a real regulatory decision.
