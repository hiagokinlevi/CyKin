## Twelve-chapter postgraduate route

This is the two-year **postgraduate course** in human factors and cyber policy. The book follows synthetic practitioner **Daniel Osei**, guided by **Dr Ingrid Holm** at the fictional **Westbrook School of Professional Studies**, redesigning the programme at the fictional **Calloway Insurance**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Decision framing, current practice and specialist evidence | lab_decide (1), lab_effect (3); EX001–EX015 |
| 4–6 | Risk and ethics, design alternatives and a controlled stepped evaluation | lab_weights (5), lab_stepped (6); EX016–EX030 |
| 7–9 | Integration, difficult cases, leadership and assurance | lab_assure (9); EX031–EX045 |
| 10–12 | Research critique, security-key project and final review | lab_keys (11); EX046–EX060 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): verification option 310k loss reduction and 1,800 staff hours vs simulations 140k and 4,500; risk difference 6.0 points (-7.1 to 19.1); combined design first in 10/10 weight variations; stepped rollout +14.4 points, permutation share 0.001; median first report 52 to 14 min; help desk 62 then 31 vs baseline 45.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Awareness and behaviour claims must cite real sources and name their limits.
- Phishing simulations only with written approval, consent at the organisational level, no punishment of individuals, aggregate reporting and support for staff; never harvest real credentials or personal data.
