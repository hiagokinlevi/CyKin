## Twelve-chapter postgraduate route

This is the two-year **postgraduate course** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic practitioner **Daniel Mensah**, tutored by **Dr. Elena Varga** at the fictional **Wexford School of Management**, improving governance at the fictional **Halbrook Mutual**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Decision-making, current practice, quantified evidence and ethics | lab_fair (3); EX001–EX020 |
| 5–8 | Design alternatives, controlled evaluation, integration and difficult cases | lab_perm (6); EX021–EX040 |
| 9–12 | Appetite and assurance, research critique, applied project and transfer | lab_appetite (9); EX041–EX060 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): supplier-breach scenario mean 170k with 68% loss-free years and 99th percentile 1,930k; tiered reviews 26.5 vs 39.3 days, permutation p = 0.0002; exceedance above appetite at 1m (8% vs 5%), 2m and 5m.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
