## Twelve-chapter postgraduate route

This is the two-year **postgraduate course** in privacy and data protection. The book follows synthetic practitioner **Priscila Andrade**, tutored by **Dr. Helen Ward** at the fictional **Ashgrove School of Management**, leading privacy at the fictional **Corvina Pay**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Decision-making, current practice, necessity evidence, risk and ethics | lab_fraud (3); EX001–EX020 |
| 5–8 | Consent design, controlled evaluation, integration and difficult cases | lab_ab (6); EX021–EX040 |
| 9–12 | Board indicators and assurance, research critique, applied retention project and transfer | lab_kri (9); EX041–EX060 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): GPS and city-level rules both detect 43.1% of synthetic fraud with 90 false alarms, device-only 56.4% with 1,572; banner acceptance 79.0% vs 57.0% (difference 22.0 points, CI 20.0–24.0), withdrawals 41 vs 12 per 1,000; three red indicators, one amber; retention project 11.0% to 3.8%.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal analysis here is teaching, not legal advice, and a synthetic DPIA or audit is never presented as a real regulatory decision.
