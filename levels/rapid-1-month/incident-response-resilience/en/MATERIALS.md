# Materials — Incident response and cyber resilience / Rapid course / English

[Edition overview](README.md) · [Chapters](SUBJECTS.md) · [One principal book plan](book/PLAN.md) · [Supplementary playbooks](../../../../playbooks/incident-response-resilience/README.md) · [Course skill](../skill/SKILL.md) · [Authored manuscript draft](book/MANUSCRIPT.md)

**Teaching and examples:** each subject-chapter belongs to the one principal book. Plans describe intended content; only the linked manuscript contains authored lessons.

**Labs and practice:** the draft has a readiness workshop in Chapter 1, two contained evidence/time labs in Chapter 2, a guided booking reconciliation and a separate read-only CSV comparison in Chapter 3, and an optional approval-route replay in Chapter 4. Each uses fictional or synthetic data and has a worked result in the book's final appendix. [Read the four chapters](book/MANUSCRIPT.md#ch-01).

**Projects and assessment:** Chapter 4 contains a staged incident tabletop, a six-artifact model handoff, an objective-to-evidence measurement contract and an original model after-action report. The book also includes a source-reading activity on the British Library's published incident review, clearly separated from the fictional Harbor Library case. These are draft activities pending independent editorial review.

**Exercises and answers:** 50 numbered exercises are drafted across the four chapters, with 50 matching worked results in the [final appendix](book/MANUSCRIPT.md#answers). The six labs and source-reading activity are supplemental; they do not inflate the numbered exercise count. The complete-book gate still requires substantial teaching, technical review and 300 verified A4 pages.

**References and glossary:** the [manuscript](book/MANUSCRIPT.md) cites primary NIST publications and the British Library's own retrospective report. The cited organizations retain their own rights; supplementary playbooks retain their source rights.
