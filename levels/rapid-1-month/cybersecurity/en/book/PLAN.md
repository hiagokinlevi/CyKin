# Principal book plan — Cybersecurity / Rapid course / English

**One book for this course and language.** Twenty English chapters are published. The older index had four subject modules. The rapid-course planning minimum is 20 lessons, and there is no maximum. Duration: 1 month; planned effort: approximately 80 hours. Audience: New learners. Entry knowledge: No prior security role; basic digital literacy. Intended outcome: recognize and explain the core risks; complete one guided task in cybersecurity.

**Editorial state:** published English manuscript; technical, language, source, and accessibility review still open. English reference edition. Not a finished qualification.

## Book requirements

Every authored chapter needs teaching, definitions, a worked example, a practical limit, an exercise and a synthesis. The final appendix holds answers keyed to the exercise IDs. Cite verifiable sources, label synthetic cases, and review for technical accuracy, rights and accessibility. There is no page quota and no exercise quota. The [book standard](../../../../../BOOK_STANDARD.md) states the publication rule. The planning minimum for this rapid book is 20 lessons, and there is no maximum.

## Primary source starting points

- [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20) — a taxonomy of security outcomes and a source for discussing organizational risk; it does not prescribe a single implementation.
- [NIST CSF 2.0 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/faqs) — explanations of framework components and the Govern function.
- [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 publication and current control-catalog update](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final) — a flexible security and privacy control catalog. Check the linked newer catalog release before citing specific controls; framework mappings are not automatic equivalences.

These sources guide the English teaching plan. The Harbor Library case is synthetic; factual claims in authored lessons need source-specific citations.

### Chapter 01 — Cybersecurity: foundations and decisions

**Stage 1; 80 planned A4 pages; 13 planned exercises.** [Read the published lesson](MANUSCRIPT.md#ch-01)

**Teaching purpose and relevance:** Give new learners a way to connect security terms to a service, its people and a bounded decision.

**Concepts and techniques:** Asset; dependency; confidentiality; integrity; availability; risk scenario; control.

**Depth and method:** At rapid course level, learners will follow a contained worked example. Use a guided case record to distinguish observations, assumptions and the next safe action.

**Worked example:** The synthetic Harbor Library booking service with reservations and an event contact list.

**Practice and exercises:** Classify observed facts and assumptions, map assets and justify one proportionate control. The planned allocation is 13 exercises; authored tasks and worked answers appear in the published book, with review still open.

**Learning sequence:** Builds on the entry knowledge and prepares Chapter 02. This is an editorial brief, not a completed lesson.
