## English route

This is the one-month **rapid course** in data governance. The book follows synthetic shift supervisor **Kwame Boateng**, mentored by **Ms. Lena Fischer** at the fictional **Riverside Adult Learning Centre**, while he organises data at the fictional **Copperleaf Café**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | First decisions, named responsibilities, rules, habits and keeping periods | EX001–EX026 |
| 3–4 | Quality, places, suppliers, the sign-up lab and the one-month plan | lab_signups; EX027–EX050 |
| 5–20 | One later Copperleaf decision each: the moved meeting, the notebook versus the print, a later leaver, an unsubscribe, the allergy review, a photo's year, a blank retention end, the second café, a busy till, a lost sheet, a supplier phone box, a payment gap, what the meeting may explain, a new USB stick, undated copies, and the leaving note | EX059–EX074 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 12 synthetic sign-up rows: 2 blank emails, 1 malformed, 2 duplicates; 5 unique valid addresses with a yes tick. Do not replace that print with 11 or 4.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real organisational or personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal and standards analysis here is teaching, not legal or certification advice, and a synthetic audit is never presented as a real assessment.
- Do not invent a staff name, a customer, an allergy, a telephone number, a bill amount, a meeting date, or a second-café pin date. Do not edit the sign-up script to force a new print.
