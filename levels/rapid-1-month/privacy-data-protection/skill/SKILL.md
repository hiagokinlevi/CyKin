## English route

This is the one-month **rapid course** in privacy and data protection. The book follows synthetic pharmacy assistant **Sofia Mendes**, mentored by **Mr. Raj Patel** at the fictional **Brightwater Community Learning Centre**, while she reviews the fictional **Juniper Pharmacy**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Personal data, health data, principles and the counter-photo rule; the signed scope and safe handling | EX001–EX026 |
| 3–4 | Systems, vendors, bases, retention, the loyalty lab and the five-action plan | lab_loyalty; EX027–EX050 |
| 5–20 | One later Juniper decision each: a later export, the lab print, unsigned clauses, blank retention, arranged shredding, the old photo, the other tills, a repeated lookup, the driver's phone, the test screenshot, a points voucher, CCTV, a stapled total, a returned phone field, customer contact, and the leaving note | EX059–EX074 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): 10 collected fields; 5 not needed for any stated purpose (50%): phone, postcode, prescription items, gender and location trail. Do not replace that print with 5 collected and 0 unneeded. The script tests field names, not values inside `purchase_history`.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Records and people are invented; no real personal data is processed.
- Cite laws by article and standards by edition; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. Legal analysis here is teaching, not legal advice, and a synthetic DPIA or audit is never presented as a real regulatory decision.
- Do not invent a customer name, a staff name, a telephone number, a takings figure, a signature date, a retention year, or a points threshold. Do not edit the loyalty script to force a new print. Do not split the figure of about 9,000 customers.
