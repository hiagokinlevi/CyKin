## Eight-chapter doctoral route

This is the two-year **doctoral research track** in security architecture and engineering. The book follows synthetic candidate **Amara Lind**, supervised by **Prof. Rafael Quintero** at the fictional **Halden Institute of Technology**, studying a threat-modelling step at the fictional **Tessaro Insurance Group**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Falsifiable question, integrity rules, coder agreement; the frontier of principles, frameworks and evidence | lab_kappa.py (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Proposal and power, baseline replication and pilot, staggered study with difference-in-differences | lab_did.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Critique and revision, synthesis and archive, defense and publication | lab_archive.py (7); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): kappa 0.79 on 40 pilot defects (agreement 0.90); DiD -0.77 per 100 changes, permutation share 0.022; archive manifest flags the edited synthetic CSV as MISMATCH.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Architecture advice must cite real standards and name its limits.
- Test or review only systems with written authorisation; report found secrets by location, never by value; never present a framework as proof of outcomes.
