## Eight-subject teaching route

This is the two-year **doctoral research track** for application and software supply chain security. The book follows synthetic doctoral candidate **Yusuf Demir** at the fictional **Eastmoor University**, supervised by **Prof. Helen Okafor** and partnered with the fictional **Larchwood Foundation**: a study of whether automated dependency-update tools shorten the time projects keep known-vulnerable versions of the fictional logging package quillog, and why some still lag.

| Subjects | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research integrity and methods; research frontier | lab_kappa.py (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Proposal, baseline replication and pilot, main study | lab_exposure.py (4), lab_bootstrap.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Peer critique, synthesis and archive, defense | lab_manifest.py (7); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic): coder agreement 0.90, chance 0.505, kappa 0.80; pilot median exposure 4.5 days with a bot vs 38 days without (5/6 vs 0/6 within a week); bootstrap difference 33.5 days, 95% interval 8.0 to 118.5 (seed 7); manifest check exposure.csv MATCH, codes.csv MISMATCH.

## Safety, privacy and information boundaries

- Synthetic code and data only; no exploit code, payloads, attack procedures or instructions for bypassing controls.
- Standard-library labs; research uses public metadata and synthetic replicas, never tests live systems.
- Incidental findings are disclosed privately through the partner's security team and reported only in aggregate.
