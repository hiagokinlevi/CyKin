## Eight-chapter doctoral route

This is the two-year **doctoral research track** in security governance, risk and compliance. The book follows synthetic candidate **Amara Lindgren**, supervised by **Prof. Rosa Delgado** at the fictional **Halvard University**, studying risk assessment at the fictional **Corvane Regional Health Board**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research integrity, assessor agreement; the research frontier | lab_kappa.py (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Proposal, matrix versus expected loss, Monte Carlo pilot, randomised main study | lab_matrix.py (3), lab_mc.py (4), lab_brier.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Critique and revision, synthesis and archive, defense and publication | lab_archive.py (7); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): kappa 0.19 between matrix assessors; matrix top risk payment fraud versus expected-loss top ransomware; Monte Carlo mean 709k with 95th percentile 4062k; Brier 0.180 matrix-band versus 0.167 calibrated; four-file archive manifest.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they score, simulate and check, and never probe real systems. Risk figures are illustrations, not benchmarks.
- Cite standards by exact edition and clause topic; paraphrase rather than reproduce copyrighted standard text. A synthetic audit, assessment or SOC 2 report is never presented as a real attestation, and compliance mappings are teaching aids, not legal advice.
