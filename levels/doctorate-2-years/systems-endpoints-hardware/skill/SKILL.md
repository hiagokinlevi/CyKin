## Eight-subject teaching route

This is the two-year **doctoral research track** for systems, endpoint and hardware security. The book follows synthetic candidate **Yusuf Adeyemi** of the fictional **Calder University**, supervised by **Prof. Ingrid Solberg**, studying whether ring-based patch deployment at the fictional **Harrowgate Health Trust** improves speed and reduces update failures compared with flat deployment.

| Subjects | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research integrity and methods, research frontier | lab_kappa.py (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Proposal, baseline replication and pilot, main study and validation | lab_pilot.py (4), lab_main.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Peer critique, synthesis and archive, defense | lab_archive.py (7); EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic): kappa 0.822 on 80 tickets (observed 0.875, chance 0.296); pilot ring medians 2, 6 and 11 days; ring 95th percentile 14.1 days and failure rate 0.011 vs flat 20.7 days and 0.028, difference 0.017 (95% CI 0.005–0.029); archive manifest 4 files, 101 bytes.

## Safety, privacy and information boundaries

- Synthetic configurations, records and data only; no exploit code, payloads, attack procedures or instructions for bypassing controls.
- Standard-library labs that only read synthetic records.
- Ticket text is redacted under approved governance; no clinical system is changed solely for research.
