## Eight-subject research route

This is the two-year **doctoral research track** for network and communications security. The book follows synthetic candidate **Ines Carvalho** of the fictional **Halden University**, supervised by **Prof. Marcus Lindqvist**, studying whether firewall rule-base complexity predicts configuration errors and whether a structured review reduces them, with the fictional partner **Westbrook Water** and two others.

| Subjects | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Research integrity, codebook and agreement; research frontier (firewall policy, detection, zero trust) | lab_kappa.py (1); EX001–EX014 |
| 3–5 | Registered proposal, baseline replication and pilot, main study with blinded validation | lab_corr.py (4), lab_shadow.py (4), lab_trial.py (5); EX015–EX032 |
| 6–8 | Peer critique, reproducibility archive, defense and publication | EX033–EX050 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic): kappa 0.860 (agreement 0.900, 4 disagreements of 40); Spearman rho 0.977 across 12 firewalls; shadowing: rule 2 by 1 (conflict), 4 by 3 (conflict), 6 by 5 (redundant); errors per 100 rules 6.17→4.35, permutation one-sided p ≈ 0.0006.

## Teaching guardrails

- Synthetic data only; no exploit code, payloads, scanning procedures or bypass steps.
- Partner configurations are pseudonymised, never published and never probed.
