**Teaching and examples:** each subject-chapter belongs to the one principal book. Plans describe intended content; only the linked manuscript contains authored lessons.

**Teaching and case:** the [English manuscript](book/MANUSCRIPT.md) drafts all 18 subjects. The fictional Lumen Library Reservations case begins with architecture, protocols, identity and evidence limits. Chapters 9–18 form a linked K workshop on collection, reversible controls, clock verification, ingest, alert evaluation, scripted triage, timeline joins, safe documentation, recovery comparison and supervised handoff. Later K15/K18 cards are staged branches, not retroactive K12 or main-case facts.

**Labs and practice:** the authored chapters use offline fixtures for dependency closure, network and identity claims, CSV rejection, byte integrity, route impact, expiry hypotheses, clock correction, bounded collection, policy predicates, multi-source intake, deterministic triage, join-quality checks, runbook stop states and security/service acceptance gates. No listing accesses a real network, account or tenant. Live collection or change remains a proposal until separately approved.

**Exercises and answers:** EX001–EX072 provide four artifacts per drafted chapter and [72 matching worked results in the same book](book/MANUSCRIPT.md#worked-results). All chapter positions have a draft start, but the volume needs substantial expansion, a 300-page A4 measurement and technical/editorial review.

**Sources and rights:** NIST and RFC sources and Python documentation support general technical concepts; all LLR, L4–L8 and K9–K18 records are fictional. Source documents retain their own rights.
