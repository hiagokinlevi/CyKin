## Eighteen-chapter technical route

This is the eighteen-month **technical course** in human factors and cyber policy. The book follows synthetic trainee **Priya Raman**, taught by **Tom Hallett** at the fictional **Eastvale Technical College**, on placement at the fictional **Kestrel Logistics**.

| Chapters | Read and teach | Labs / exercises |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Systems, networks, identities, scripting and scope | lab_unlocked (3), lab_weekly (4); EX001–EX020 |
| 6–9 | System model, telemetry, failure modes and methods | lab_steps (6), lab_survey (9); EX021–EX036 |
| 10–15 | Configuration, verification, contained lab I, troubleshooting, automation and integration | lab_verify (11), lab_contained (12), lab_triage (14); EX037–EX060 |
| 16–18 | Documentation and safety, contained lab II and supervised project | lab_feedback (17), lab_drivers (18); EX061–EX072 |

Verified lab facts (synthetic unless cited): unlocked minutes 1,920 to 85; 21 of 100 suspicious messages become replied reports; top barrier 88/210 = 42%; 5 of 7 controls pass first time; old route 260 s vs 22 s; 12 of 80 reports need human review; feedback 26/30 vs 17/30; driver reports 6 to 19, median 95 to 12 min.

## Teaching guardrails

- Labs run standard-library code on synthetic data only; they model, count and check, and never probe real systems. Awareness and behaviour claims must cite real sources and name their limits.
- Phishing simulations only with written approval, consent at the organisational level, no punishment of individuals, aggregate reporting and support for staff; never harvest real credentials or personal data.
