## Incident Response primary-source leads

The technical Incident Response book's expanded Chapters 1–5 cite NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 for response context; RFC 8446 and RFC 9110 for the specific TLS and HTTP concepts; RFC 4180 and Python's CSV documentation for the L4-Q quoted-record lesson; Python's hashlib documentation for byte-digest mechanics; and older NIST SP 800-86/800-92 only for historical forensic/log-management concepts. The L1-V, N2-V, L3-V, L4-Q and L5-B records, interpretations and expected outputs are **original fictional teaching material**, not observations or algorithms from those publications. The CSV logical-record and 43-to-45-byte newline variants were executed locally. A specialist source and technical review of the full book remains pending.

The nine English Incident Response book plans now link [NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final) for the current response and risk-management framework, [NIST SP 800-184](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/184/final) for recovery, [NIST SP 800-84](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/84/final) for exercises, and [NIST SP 800-86](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/86/final) for forensic methods. The rapid manuscript cites Rev. 3's RS.AN-06 and RS.AN-07 for the integrity, provenance, access and retention of response records; its preservation queue is an original teaching model, not a NIST-prescribed form. It also cites [NIST SP 800-92](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/92/final) for high-level log-management background; its delayed-arrival fixture and algorithm are original teaching examples. NIST identifies Rev. 3 of SP 800-61 as superseding Rev. 2. SP 800-84, SP 800-86 and SP 800-92 date from 2006; their procedures and tool assumptions need current verification when each lesson is authored. These links are starting points, not a completed bibliography or a claim that NIST content is covered by CyKin's CC0 dedication.

## Cybersecurity primary-source leads

The English Cybersecurity plans link the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0](https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20), its [official FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/faqs) and the [NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 publication and control-catalog update notice](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/53/r5/upd1/final). The CSF provides a taxonomy of outcomes; SP 800-53 provides a flexible catalog of controls. The NIST publication page points to a newer control-catalog release, so chapter authors must verify the exact release before citing a control. Mappings between these sources do not establish automatic equivalence. The Harbor Library learning case is fictional. Authored chapters must cite the relevant primary source beside each factual claim; these links do not license NIST text as CyKin content.

In this sample, none of the 13 files contained a direct external Markdown source link. That is a **citation gap in the sampled files**, not proof that whole repositories lack references. The small sample lengths and operational emphasis make them useful starting points for labs and cases; they do not meet the book standard by themselves. Before reuse, check commands, versions, rights, accessibility, examples, and claims against primary sources.

## Integration decision

Keep the 13 repositories as linked source projects and the CyKin course tree as the curriculum home. Place relevant source playbooks in each area's separate practice index, linked to an exact source revision. The book remains the principal teaching material. Write original explanations, safe exercises, rubrics and answers in the matching book. Do not copy CC BY material into a CC0-only book without a rights decision. Track the source path and reviewed commit in the book references when material is adapted.
