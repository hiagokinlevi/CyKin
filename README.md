## Courses, weekly chapters and daily lessons

The shelf above is one percentage per academic area. This section is the structure those percentages sit on. Read it before treating 4.3% and 100% as the same kind of number.

**How the library is built**

1. **Area.** The collection has 23 academic areas. Cybersecurity is one of them. Each area is taught at every formation level.
2. **Course.** One area at one level is one course, with one course skill. Nine levels × 23 areas = **207 courses**.
3. **Book.** One course in one language is one principal book (`book/MANUSCRIPT.md`). Nine languages give **1,863 planned books**. English is written and reviewed first. A translation is its own book and keeps its own editorial state.
4. **Subject module.** Subjects organize the inside of a book. The older index uses 4, 12, 18, 30, 40, 50, 12, 8 and 8 modules, which is 182 modules for one area across the nine levels. Most English manuscripts still contain one chapter for each of those modules.
5. **Weekly chapter.** The default book groups one instructional week in one chapter.
6. **Weekday lesson.** A weekly chapter usually holds five lessons, Monday through Friday. One lesson is the teaching, one worked example, one exercise the learner can attempt alone, a worked answer in the final appendix, and previous / index / next links. A chapter may hold a different number of lessons when the subject requires it.

There is no maximum chapter, lesson, word or page size. Add a lesson when the idea needs it. Do not add an empty lesson to reach a count. Longer courses have more weekly chapters and need deeper teaching. Level names are study plans, not accredited degrees.

### One book, not one book per week

~~~text
Area -> level-specific course -> selected language edition
  -> ONE principal MANUSCRIPT.md
       -> weekly chapters organized by curricular modules
            -> daily lessons: teaching + worked example + exercise
       -> final appendix: explained answers for those exercises
  -> ONE course skill shared by the language editions
~~~

The arrows mean organization and containment, not separate files or proof of publication. For **Cybersecurity**, nine levels mean **nine principal books per language**, or **81 planned book editions across nine languages**. Its engineering course has one book per language with a baseline of 260 weekly chapters and 1,300 daily lessons; it is not 260 books. Keep answers in the same principal book. All nine levels together give 1,044 weekly chapters and 5,220 lessons per area and language. These are planning totals, not translations already delivered.

### Check actual study dates as well as the baseline

Under the [documented calendar convention](STUDY_CALENDAR.md), **13 October to 13 November 2026, end excluded, contains 23 Monday–Friday dates**. A 20-lesson edition therefore leaves three dated positions needing an explicit instructional allocation. Holidays and vacations are not silently removed; special calendars need stated exceptions. Additional days require genuine teaching, examples and assessed practice, not repeated exercises or empty placeholders. Source-indexed lessons, required dates and learner completion remain different measures. A calendar or README change is not a verified website deployment.

## How the study site works

The [OpenCyber Academy website](https://opencyber.academy/) is an in-site reader for this public curriculum. Students choose a language and browse **by level** or **by subject**, then open one separate course page for a level–area combination. The course page shows its current modules, published chapters, exercises, worked answers and previous/index/next navigation; the full principal book is also readable on the site. The planning minimum is shown separately from the chapters actually published, and it is not a maximum. A plan or an untranslated outline is never a completed lesson. The site interface starts in English, detects supported browser languages, and lets readers switch among English, Portuguese and Spanish; each book's actual language and editorial state remain independent of that interface choice.

The home page has an interactive [23-area learning map](https://opencyber.academy/#learning-map) in the same order as the public curriculum. Its four visible phases explain how foundational decisions lead into engineering, operations and critical applications. The highlight moves automatically, can be paused and respects reduced-motion preferences. Every area opens a separate subject route and its nine level choices. The suggested order helps orientation; learners may enter at any area.

## How this library is organized

1. **Formation level** — choose one of nine pathways below.
2. **Academic area** — every level has all 23 areas, each represented by one course home.
3. **Language edition** — every course has one planned principal book per language. English is authored and reviewed first.
4. **Weekly chapters and daily lessons** — subjects remain modules. The default book then groups about five weekday lessons in one weekly chapter. Each lesson has teaching, a worked example, an exercise and a worked answer. The [structure table](#courses-weekly-chapters-and-daily-lessons) separates that plan from the legacy module index and from the shelf, the course matrix and the lesson inventory.
5. **One course skill** — a single `SKILL.md` guides AI clients through the whole course and its chapters. Playbooks are supplementary.

~~~text
levels/<level>/<area>/
├── README.md               course home: objectives, all languages and links
├── skill/SKILL.md          one skill for this course
└── <language>/
    ├── README.md           edition and editorial status
    ├── SUBJECTS.md         modules, weekly chapters, daily lessons and links
    ├── MATERIALS.md        labs, cases, exercises and references
    └── book/
        ├── PLAN.md          outlines and learning sequence; not authored lessons
        └── MANUSCRIPT.md    actual lessons, examples, exercises and final answers
~~~
