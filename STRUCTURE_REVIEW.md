## What a reader can use now

All nine [Incident Response course levels](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/README.md) now have distinct subject sequences, with shared foundations retained where appropriate. All **182 English chapter briefs** specify a distinct purpose, concepts, synthetic example, activity and learning sequence. The nine English plans also link [primary-source starting points](SOURCE_AUDIT.md#incident-response-primary-source-leads); full chapter bibliographies remain pending. The [rapid book draft](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) has initial teaching for all four subjects, 50 exercises and worked results. Chapter 1 explains the preparation-versus-case distinction in NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3, separates response recommendation from approval, execution and verification, and includes a bounded 09:05 decision gate and readiness workshop. Chapter 2 distinguishes event, discovery, ingestion, query-run and collection times; it adds a bounded query-coverage example, two synthetic labs, a separate delayed-arrival T2 replay and a three-variant evidence-package peer audit, and a separate cross-source correlation Q1 fixture with overlapping identity sessions, explicit and missing keys, and a six-point rubric. All four published Chapter 2 Python examples and their expected outputs were run locally. Chapter 3 compares bounded containment options, adds a separate W1 test of a proposed write pause across two routes, teaches competing cause hypotheses and a cause-to-fix verification contract with an expanded EX032 answer, distinguishes RTO/RPO targets from demonstrated recovery, and includes guided booking reconciliation, a read-only CSV comparison and an optional three-candidate isolated-restore replay. The replay separately screens provenance, byte integrity, data-point age, booking correctness and service acceptance; its matrix and worked result do not claim a production restore. The W1 classifier and changed-state variant were checked locally, as were the two-part Python comparison listing and its worked output. Chapter 3 also compares the fictional case with the [British Library's cited 2024 incident review](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/99206a2d1e9f07b35712b78f7d75fbb09560c08d.pdf/british-library-cyber-incident-review-8-march-2024.pdf?dl=), explicitly separating reported facts from teaching inferences. R17's wording was corrected so the training owner does not anticipate the later B20 edit. Chapter 4 releases later evidence in sequence, includes a claim ledger, a six-artifact tabletop handoff, a measurement contract and an original model after-action report. Separate approval-route and audience-specific communication replays have worked results; the latter distinguishes internal coordination, a patron-facing holding draft and a privacy assessment request without treating an unattributed exposure claim as a finding. R21's tolerable pause is no longer described as formal service-owner approval. All **50 numbered exercises** now have objectives, inputs, required artifacts, assessment criteria and expanded worked results; the Chapter 4 answers respect card-release order. The [subject index](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/SUBJECTS.md) links the course skill and marks all four chapters as drafts. The rapid lessons still need substantial expansion and technical review. The [professional draft](levels/professional-6-months/incident-response-resilience/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) now authors ten sequential subjects with a fictional Cedar Training Network case, 50 exercises, matching answers and reproducible read-only fixtures. Chapters 6–10 add containment, eradication, recovery, communication and playbook review. Its two remaining chapters and ten planned exercises are pending. The other seven Incident Response books have plans, not manuscripts.

The latest Chapter 1 addition turns the fictional service map into a D1 evidence-readiness decision card and a fully worked six-field proposed test record. It names proposed sources, ownership and bounded checks for booking state, session association, user-facing harm and restoration. A falsifiable rehearsal includes both an expected event and an out-of-range control; the lesson also traces missing-record causes and privacy limits. D1 is explicitly a preparation plan, not an R-series observation or proof that those sources existed at the 09:05 case checkpoint.

The latest Chapter 2 addition turns R9–R16 into a four-request preservation queue. It teaches decision value, possible record loss, authority, privacy and source fidelity; distinguishes the booking-audit R9 source from the application-audit R14 summary; and leaves the precise anchor of R15's 24-hour rule unknown. EX018 and its worked result now require a bounded two-source comparison. The queue and any hypothetical copy remain separate from observed case evidence.

The latest Chapter 3 addition makes the eradication decision testable. It compares credential/session misuse, an application authorization fault and an incomplete approval record without declaring any one a diagnosis. The reader designs a bounded discriminator, conditional fix, positive and negative synthetic tests, monitoring and a re-open condition. EX032 and its final answer now assess two competing mechanisms; no proposed test is reported as performed.

An internal structural pass found no duplicate chapter title within any Incident Response course, matched all 182 briefs to their grade and checked the planned study hours, pages and exercise allocations. Specialist review of the proposed depth and cases is still pending. ## Publication and rights

Original maintainer-owned text is dedicated under [CC0 1.0](LICENSE), subject to [rights exceptions](RIGHTS.md). Linked third-party material and independent repositories retain their own rights. The [book standard](BOOK_STANDARD.md) requires an original complete manuscript, at least 50 substantial exercises with worked answers, technical and editorial review, accessible Markdown and at least 300 actual A4 pages in a fixed verification layout. No current draft passes that gate.
