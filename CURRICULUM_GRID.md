# Weekly chapters and daily lessons by level

Each academic area offers one course at each of nine levels and one principal book per language edition. Curricular subjects organize modules. The default book groups **five daily lessons in one weekly chapter**. Every instructional weekday has a distinct lesson with technical teaching, a worked example, a substantive exercise and a worked answer. The counts below are **minimum planning baselines, never upper limits**: add lessons, chapters and time when depth requires them. Longer programs have more weekly chapters and need deeper treatment. The baselines apply to `en`, `pt`, `es`, `he`, `fr`, `ru`, `de`, `zh` and `ja`; they are not claims that those editions are already authored or accredited.

The normalized baseline uses four weeks for the rapid month, 26 for six months, 78 for 18 months and 52 per year for longer tracks. These are **instructional weeks**, independent of start date, holidays and vacations; actual study pace may vary, and additional lessons can extend the schedule. Longer courses should become larger through substantive teaching, not an artificial page quota. A4 renderings are measured and reported separately. See [the book standard](BOOK_STANDARD.md).

Most existing plans and manuscripts still use earlier subject-chapter counts (4, 12, 18, 30, 40, 50, 12, 8, 8). Those counts are **legacy module coverage**, not weekly-chapter or daily-lesson coverage. The [rapid Incident Response English book](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/SUBJECTS.md) is an edition-specific example of four weekly chapters containing 20 authored daily lessons; it does not change another edition automatically. During migration, preserve authored content and show current chapter and lesson availability separately from the minimum baselines. Complete and review the English collection before translating it into Portuguese, Spanish and the remaining editions. A translated plan is not a published lesson.

## Dated courses must account for every required weekday

The [study-calendar contract](STUDY_CALENDAR.md) complements these instructional baselines without replacing them. To promise one lesson on every Monday–Friday date throughout a stated civil-month duration, count the actual dates from the first study weekday to the exclusive calendar-month anniversary. Show weekend-start adjustment, month-end clamping and any explicit calendar exceptions. Do not silently remove holidays, repeat a task, skip an unwritten day or truncate additional lessons. A missing date allocation requires substantive authored teaching or an openly revised schedule before full dated coverage can be claimed.

The website calendar keeps baseline, required dates, source-indexed daily lessons, missing allocations and extensions separate for the selected language. Legacy chapter counts and source errors do not become daily-completion claims. A partial first calendar week is not a new book-chapter boundary. Read the [implementation and test limits](STUDY_CALENDAR.md#implementation-and-bounded-verification): source publication still requires a separately validated local build and deployment before the new page is live.
