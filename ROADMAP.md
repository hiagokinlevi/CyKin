# CyberABC learning and editorial roadmap

**Authorship and editorial responsibility:** [hiagokinlevi](https://github.com/hiagokinlevi). This is the public learner-facing plan. Construction tools and migration records remain in the private CyberABC Engine.

## Current curriculum

The [academic map](AREA_MAP.md) has **23 broad areas in five schools**. Every [level](levels/README.md) contains all 23 area courses, each with nine language editions: **207 distinct course designs** and **1,863 planned language editions**. Each course has one principal book per language, with one curricular subject per chapter. English has **4,186 planned subjects** and **207 course skills**; 336 English briefs now have individual teaching outlines and 3,850 remain working summaries. The other language plans await translation. Four English manuscript drafts are public, and **no book is yet complete**. See the [progress review](STRUCTURE_REVIEW.md).

## English production, beginning with Incident Response

Author sequential batches of **five to ten subject-chapters** when that many remain in a course. Before publishing each batch, review transitions, evidence release, technical depth, exercise/answer correspondence and course-skill guidance. A final smaller remainder, such as professional Chapters 11–12, closes that course's sequence without being presented as a new five-chapter batch.

1. **Write and validate the books in sequential batches.** The [rapid Incident Response draft](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) has four authored subjects, 50 exercises and answers, and a 105-page A4 preflight. Its Chapter 4 includes evidence-linked score calibration. The [professional Incident Response draft](levels/professional-6-months/incident-response-resilience/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) has the first ten of twelve subjects, with a continuous fictional case and EX001–EX050 plus worked answers; its hash-checked A4 preflight is 51 pages. Chapters 6–10 cover containment, eradication, recovery, communication and playbook quality. Both books need substantive expansion, specialist review, editing and at least 300 verified A4 pages before release. The next writing remainder is professional Chapters 11–12, followed by deeper review of both books and the remaining Incident Response levels.
2. Refine the English chapter plans alongside book writing. The tailored Incident Response set has 182 briefs; Cybersecurity has 154 of 182. Its remaining 28 advanced briefs and the other 3,822 English briefs are editorial work, but they do not substitute for authored lessons. Resolve the Incident Response specialist-review findings as the first book advances.
3. Continue the [Incident Response area](levels/rapid-1-month/incident-response-resilience/README.md) from the professional draft through technical, technologist, bachelor's, engineering, postgraduate, master's and doctoral research levels. All nine levels now have subject-specific English grids; review their technical depth and dependencies while writing each distinct book.
4. Work through the remaining 22 areas in English, maintaining one distinct book per course. The earlier [Cybersecurity](levels/rapid-1-month/cybersecurity/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) and [Security Governance](levels/rapid-1-month/security-governance-risk-compliance/en/book/MANUSCRIPT.md) drafts remain available but do not change the new writing priority.
5. Review and edit the **entire English set** for technical accuracy, progression, source rights, exercises, accessibility and language. Render each book to A4 and verify the [completion gate](BOOK_STANDARD.md) before changing its status to complete.
